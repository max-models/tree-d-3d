tree-d-3d computes the electrostatic field of N charges in a plane, in free space, and moves
the charges in it, with an optional uniform magnetic field $B_z$. Nothing is put on a mesh: the
field is a sum over the charges, made fast by a Barnes-Hut tree walk or, for line charges, a
fast multipole method, and conductors enter as charged panels on their surfaces. Two
interaction kernels, chosen per tree with `kernel=`:

- `"coulomb"`: point charges with the 3D Coulomb interaction, confined to the plane;
- `"log"`: infinite line charges along $z$, the Green's function of 2D Poisson (2D Cartesian
  PIC).

Each equation on these pages is declared on the code that implements it, as a method card, and
checked by the tests: eighteen closed forms against SymPy rules, five discretisations against
their order of convergence. The pages of the section list the cards module by module; this one
shows how they fit together.

## The pipeline

1. **[Kernels](/tree-d-3d/numerics/flat-tree/).** The exact softened sums of both kernels,
   $\phi = \sum_j q_j/\sqrt{r^2 + \varepsilon^2}$ and
   $\phi = -\sum_j q_j \ln(r^2 + \varepsilon^2)$, define the model; `direct_field` evaluates
   them in $O(NM)$.
2. **[Tree walk](/tree-d-3d/numerics/flat-tree/).** A quadtree, built level by level, carries
   the moments of every cell about its centre. The walk replaces a cell that looks small enough
   from the target by its expansion, through the quadrupole for point charges and to order $p$
   in complex form for line charges, in $O(\log N)$ per target:

<MethodCard name="tree_d_3d.flat_tree.FlatQuadtree.evaluate" headingLevel={3} />

3. **[Fast multipole method](/tree-d-3d/numerics/flat-tree/)** for line charges: a dual-tree
   traversal pairs well-separated cells, whose multipoles become local expansions (M2L),
   shifted down the tree (L2L) and evaluated at the particles (L2P), in $O(N)$:

<MethodCard name="tree_d_3d.flat_tree.FlatQuadtree.fmm" headingLevel={3} />

4. **[Macroparticles](/tree-d-3d/numerics/bodies/)** carry a weight on mass and charge, so
   $q/m$ is kept.
5. **[Particle pusher](/tree-d-3d/numerics/simulation/):** an electric half-kick, the exact
   gyration in $B_z$ over the step, another half-kick; second order, velocity Verlet at
   $B_z = 0$. Absorbing walls and `simgeometry` objects remove the particles that cross them.
6. **Boundaries.** Two grounded plates by a [conformal map](/tree-d-3d/numerics/strip/) of the
   strip onto the half-plane, where one image per charge makes the plates equipotentials, and
   conductors at prescribed potentials by the
   [boundary-integral (panel) method](/tree-d-3d/numerics/electrodes/): an induced line-charge
   density on each surface, piecewise constant on straight panels, collocated at their
   midpoints. The free charges of a meshless space-charge model (a gun, a beam) enter both as
   sources.

The [reference tree](/tree-d-3d/numerics/reference/) is the walk of step 2 as a readable
object tree. Two codes stand beside the pipeline: the
[smooth-membership FMM](/tree-d-3d/numerics/smooth/), a NumPy prototype whose forces are the
exact gradient of a smooth energy, and the classic gravitational
[Barnes-Hut code](/tree-d-3d/numerics/gravity/).

## Units and conventions

The kernels return raw potentials and fields; the callers multiply by
$k_e = 1/(4\pi\varepsilon_0)$ (`K_E`) or by the `k_e` they are given, so SI inputs give SI
outputs. Line charges are per unit length (C/m), and their potential is defined up to a
constant: `reference_radius` $r_0$ makes it $-2k_e q \ln(r/r_0)$. Complex notation
$z = x + iy$, with $E_x - iE_y$ the conjugate field. Per-particle arrays with a component axis
are component-major, $(2, N)$.

## Not in the model

- No 3D geometry or octree yet: positions, fields and trees are planar.
- No FMM for the Coulomb kernel, no Coulomb multipoles beyond the quadrupole, no M2M (moments
  are summed per node), no error estimate or automatic choice of $\theta$ and $p$.
- Softening only in the direct pairs: the far cells of the log tree and the FMM are
  unsoftened (the smooth-membership FMM has a compact softening with an exact far field).
- No periodic boundaries, dielectrics or Neumann conditions; no conductor at infinity; the
  electrode system is solved directly (no FMM-accelerated GMRES); no electrodes inside
  `ElectrostaticSimulation` (pair it with `BoundaryIntegralElectrodes` by hand).
- No magnetic self-fields, induction, time-dependent or non-uniform $B$; no relativity,
  radiation, collisions or emission in the library (the examples add some of them).

## Verification

Besides the checks of the cards, the tests compare the flat tree with the reference tree node
for node, every compiled kernel with its Python source and its NumPy, CUDA (emulated) and Metal
versions, the walk and the FMM with the direct sum against $\theta$ and $p$, the exact cyclotron
orbit, energy and momentum over many steps, a self-similarly expanding disk of line charges,
the grounded circle, the cylindrical capacitor and an L-shaped conductor against their closed
forms, and the strip against charged sheets.
